# Course 18: Professional Presence & Opportunity Building

## Lesson 3: Showcase Your Work

---

### What You're Doing Today

You already did the hard part back in Course 16 — you built real projects and wrote case studies about them. Today you make that work *visible*. You'll pick your 2–3 strongest projects and add them to your LinkedIn Featured section (or your portfolio page's Projects section, if you're using the portfolio-first path). Then you'll run the Portfolio Review Checklist on your own profile/page as a final polish before anyone else sees it.

### Why This Matters

A recruiter, mentor, or program reviewer looking at your profile decides whether to keep reading in the first few seconds. If your best work is buried, invisible, or unexplained, it doesn't count — it might as well not exist. A **showcase entry** is how you make sure your best work is the first thing a stranger sees, and that they understand it without needing to ask you a single question.

---

### How to Present a Project as a Showcase Entry

Every showcase entry has exactly three parts. Skipping any one of them weakens the whole entry.

| Part | What it does | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Names the project and what kind of thing it is | Short — project name plus a 2–4 word type tag, e.g. "(App Concept)" or "(Website Build)" |
| **Result-focused description** | Tells a stranger what happened because of this project, not just what you did | 1–2 sentences. Include a real number or outcome if you have one (users, testers, time saved, a problem solved) |
| **Link** | Sends the viewer to real proof | A working, publicly viewable link to your case study doc, deck, image, or live project |

**Result-focused vs. task-focused — the key difference:**

- Task-focused (weak): *"I made a budgeting app using Canva for my class project."*
- Result-focused (strong): *"Designed a budgeting app prototype that 8 beta testers used for two weeks; 6 said it helped them track spending more consistently."*

The second version answers "so what?" The first one doesn't.

### Worked Example

**Fictional student:** Jordan R., 16, completed a Course 16 case study called "MoodBoard: A Daily Mood Tracker for Teens" — a mobile app mockup, tested with 10 classmates over one week.

**Showcase entry:**

- **Title:** MoodBoard — Daily Mood Tracker (App Concept)
- **Description:** Designed and mocked up a mood-tracking app aimed at teens; tested with 10 classmates over one week, with 8 saying it helped them notice mood patterns they hadn't before.
- **Link:** `https://docs.google.com/[Jordan's shared case study link]`

Notice: the title is short and specific, the description has a real number and a real outcome, and the link points straight at evidence — not just a claim.

### Step-by-Step Activity

1. **Open your Course 16 case study/project docs.** Reread them and rank your projects — which 2–3 have the clearest outcome or result attached?
2. **Draft your showcase entries on the Worksheet** for this lesson (title, description, link) before you touch your live profile. Drafting first means you won't publish a weak entry by accident.
3. **Check your link's sharing settings.** In Google Docs or Canva, set sharing to "Anyone with the link can view," then test the link in a private/incognito browser window to confirm a stranger could actually open it.
4. **Add the entry to your profile/page:**
   - *LinkedIn (16+):* Add profile section → Recommended → Add featured → Add a link → paste your case study link → edit the title and description fields to match your worksheet draft → Save.
   - *Portfolio-first (under 16):* Open your portfolio page → go to the Projects section → add a new entry block with title, description, and a "View Project" link → confirm the link is publicly viewable.
5. **View your profile/page as a stranger would.** On LinkedIn, use "View as public." On a portfolio page, open the link in an incognito window. Confirm the entry looks right and the link works.
6. **Run the Portfolio Review Checklist** (`Shared_Resources/Checklists/Portfolio_Review_Checklist.md`) against your whole profile/page. Make at least two edits based on what it turns up.

### Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Featured section** | The part of a LinkedIn profile built to highlight a small number of hand-picked posts, links, or media — not a full archive of everything you've done |
| **Showcase (entry)** | A single title + result-focused description + link, presenting one project as proof of a skill or outcome |
| **Result-focused description** | A description that states what changed or who was affected because of the project, rather than only listing the steps taken to build it |
