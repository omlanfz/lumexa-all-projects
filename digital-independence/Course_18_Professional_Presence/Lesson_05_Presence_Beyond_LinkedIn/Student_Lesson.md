# Lesson 5: Build a Professional Presence Beyond LinkedIn

## What This Lesson Is About

LinkedIn has an age minimum, and even if you're old enough, you might not have it set up yet — and that's completely fine. A real professional presence doesn't depend on one platform. It depends on whether the people who matter (teachers, mentors, competition judges, internship coordinators, scholarship reviewers, future employers) can find real, credible proof of what you can do.

**This lesson's core idea: a strong portfolio-first presence — built on your Course 16 site plus a few other channels — is not a smaller or "junior" version of a professional presence. It is equally credible.** A live project someone can actually see and try tells an evaluator more than a network profile ever could.

## Why This Matters

Adults who evaluate teenagers are not expecting a polished LinkedIn network. They're expecting to see specific, real, checkable evidence: a project you built, a competition you entered, a showcase you were part of, growth they can trace over time. Every student can build that, starting today, regardless of age or platform access.

## Presence Channels Beyond LinkedIn

| Channel | What it is | Why it builds credibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Portfolio site** (your Course 16 site) | A site you fully control, showing your projects, skills, and how to reach you | Direct evidence of real work — the single most important channel you own |
| **Content/notes section** | A simple, ongoing log on your portfolio site — short updates about what you're building and learning | Shows growth over time and recent activity, not a one-time snapshot |
| **School-sanctioned showcases & competitions** | Science fairs, hackathons, robotics leagues, app challenges, art/writing contests, class exhibitions | Third-party recognition — someone outside you vouched for the work |
| **Community project boards** | Places built for sharing specific kinds of work: GitHub for code, Behance for design, a class-wide showcase page or Padlet | Discoverability within a community of people who understand the work |
| **Referral networks** | Teacher recommendations, school mentorship programs, alumni-to-student introduction systems | Personal vouching from someone with credibility of their own |

You don't need every channel. You need a **primary channel** (usually your portfolio site) plus **one or two supporting channels** that reinforce it.

## Worked Example: Alex T., age 15

Alex is building a presence around web development, without LinkedIn.

- **Portfolio site:** `alext.lumexa.dev` — home page with a one-line bio, a projects page with two real sites Alex built (a chess-timer app and a local animal shelter's volunteer sign-up page), each with a short write-up of the problem solved and tools used.
- **Content/notes section:** Every two weeks, a short entry like *"Sept 1: Rebuilt the shelter sign-up form so it works on phones. Took a while to figure out why the layout broke on small screens — turned out to be a missing CSS unit."*
- **School showcase:** The shelter sign-up project was entered in Alex's school's community-impact tech fair and won "Most Useful Project."
- **Community project board:** Both projects are uploaded to Alex's class GitHub organization, where classmates and teachers can browse them.

Nowhere in this presence does Alex need LinkedIn. An evaluator looking at this sees real projects, visible growth, and outside recognition — everything a strong presence requires.

## Step-by-Step Activity

1. **Open your Course 16 portfolio site.** Check that it has: a bio, at least one real project with a description, and a way to contact you.
2. **Add or update your content/notes section.** Write two short entries (2–4 sentences each) about work you've actually done. Use this starter if you're stuck: *"This week I worked on ___. The hardest part was ___. Next I want to try ___."*
3. **Pick two channels beyond your primary one.** Look at the Presence Channels Map above. Choose one channel to strengthen this month (for example: submit a project to a school showcase, upload a project to a community board, or ask a teacher about a referral/mentorship program).
4. **Write one concrete next action for each channel you picked**, with a realistic date (this week or this month — not "eventually").
5. **Bring your updated site and your two next actions to class** for discussion and instructor review.

## Key Terms

- **Channel:** Any specific place or way that people can discover your work and skills — a website, a showcase, a competition, a community board.
- **Credibility:** How trustworthy your presence is to someone evaluating it — built through real evidence, specificity, and outside recognition, not through how many platforms you're on.
- **Discoverability:** How easy it is for the right person to actually find your work — a clear link, a searchable name, a listed project — regardless of which platform hosts it.
