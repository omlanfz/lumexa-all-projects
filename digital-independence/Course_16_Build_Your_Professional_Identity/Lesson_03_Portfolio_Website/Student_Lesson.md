# Lesson 3: Build Your Portfolio Website

## What You'll Learn
How to structure and build a real, working portfolio website using HTML and CSS — the same
site that will hold your bio, brand, and project case studies by the end of this course.

## Why It Matters
A portfolio site is proof you can point to. A résumé says "I can build websites." A live
site someone can click through is the difference between a claim and evidence.

## The 5 Sections Every Portfolio Needs
1. **Intro / Hero** — your name + one-line tagline about your direction
2. **About** — a short paragraph (your real bio comes in Lesson 4)
3. **Skills** — a simple list or grid of things you can do
4. **Projects** — cards for your case studies (real content comes in Lessons 4 & 6)
5. **Contact** — a safe way to reach you (school email or a contact form — never your home address or phone number)

## Step-by-Step
1. Open the `starter/` project folder in your code editor.
2. Open `index.html` — find the 5 section comments (`<!-- HERO -->`, `<!-- ABOUT -->`, etc.).
3. Replace the placeholder name and tagline in the Hero section with your own.
4. Fill in a short temporary About paragraph (you'll rewrite this properly in Lesson 4).
5. Add 2–3 skills to the Skills grid based on your Lesson 2 worksheet.
6. Leave Projects as placeholder cards for now — real case studies come in Lessons 4 & 6.
7. Preview your page in the browser and check it at different window widths.

## Key Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Semantic HTML | Using tags that describe meaning (`<header>`, `<section>`) instead of only generic `<div>`s |
| Responsive | A layout that adapts to different screen sizes |
| Local server | A way to preview your site on your own computer before it's live on the internet |
| Skeleton | The basic structure of a page before real content and styling are finished |
