# Lesson 1: How the Freelance Economy Works

## What This Lesson Is About

You already have skills — maybe you're good at design, writing, coding, editing video, tutoring, or organizing things. This lesson is about the moment those skills turn into something someone else will pay for. That moment has a name: the **freelance economy**. By the end of this lesson, you'll understand how it works, what it looks like in real life, and what the honest landscape of platforms and opportunities looks like — without signing up for anything today.

## Why It Matters

Almost every adult who does creative, technical, or specialized work has, at some point, done freelance or service work — even people who mostly have a regular job. Understanding how this works now means that when you're ready to actually offer a service (this year or years from now), you'll know how to think about it instead of guessing.

---

## What Is the Freelance Economy?

The **freelance economy** is the pattern of people offering specific skills as one-time or short-term services to clients, instead of being hired long-term by a single employer. It's not one company or app — it happens across every industry: a photographer hired for one wedding, a coder hired to fix one bug, a tutor hired for one exam's worth of sessions.

### The Value-Exchange Chain: Skill → Service → Payment

This is the core idea of the whole lesson. A skill only turns into income once it passes through three stages:

1. **Skill** — something you can do. Example: "I can edit video."
2. **Service** — that skill turned into a specific, defined thing a client can actually buy. Example: "I will edit a 3-minute highlight video from your raw footage, with music and captions, delivered within 5 days."
3. **Payment** — money exchanged for the completed service, agreed on *before* the work starts.

Most people get stuck at step 1. They know they're good at something but never turn it into step 2 — a clear, specific service. That's the skill this whole course builds.

### Freelance/Service Work vs. Employment

| | Employment | Freelance / Service Work |
|---|---|---|
| Who you work for | One employer, ongoing | Different clients, project by project |
| Schedule | Usually regular hours | Work starts and stops per project |
| Pay | Regular paycheck | Paid per project or task, agreed in advance |
| Tools | Usually provided by employer | Usually your own |
| For minors | Often has direct legal protections (e.g., minimum wage) | Almost always requires a parent/guardian involved in the account and payments |

Neither one is "better" — they're different systems with different rules. This course focuses on the freelance/service side because it's a place where a skill you already have, today, can become something real.

## Labeled Example: Mara and the Logo Request

Here's a real-shaped (fictional) example, labeled so you can see the chain clearly.

> Mara, 17, is asked by Denise, a neighbor starting a dog-walking business, to design a simple logo.

- **Skill**: Mara knows how to design logos in Canva.
- **Service** (defined after Mara asks Denise what she actually needs): "Two logo concepts, one round of revisions, delivered as a PNG and a vector file."
- **Client**: Denise.
- **Scope**: exactly what's included — 2 concepts, 1 revision, 2 file types. Nothing outside that is included unless they agree to more.
- **Payment**: $40 flat, agreed before Mara starts working, paid when the final files are delivered — to an account her parent manages, since Mara is a minor.

Notice: the price and the scope were both settled *before* any design work happened. That single habit — agree first, then work — is the most important thing to take from this lesson.

---

## Key Terms

- **Freelance** — working on a project-by-project basis for different clients, rather than being a long-term employee of one employer.
- **Client** — the person or business paying for a service.
- **Scope** — the exact, agreed-on boundaries of what a service includes (and doesn't include).
- **Service** — a skill packaged into a specific, deliverable outcome a client can pay for.
- **Marketplace** — a website or app (like Fiverr, Upwork, Etsy, or Gumroad) where clients and freelancers find each other and often handle payment through the platform.

## In-Class Activity: Label Your Own Example

**Step 1.** Think of one skill you have (or would like to build) — writing, design, coding, tutoring, editing, organizing, translating, etc.

**Step 2.** In a new Google Doc or Notion page, write a short (2–4 sentence) made-up scenario where someone asks you to help with something using that skill. Give the "client" a name.

**Step 3.** Label four things in your scenario, the same way Mara's example was labeled above:
- **Skill** — what you can do.
- **Service** — the specific, defined thing you'd actually deliver (not just the skill).
- **Scope** — what exactly is included (how many rounds of revision, what format, what's *not* included).
- **Payment** — a reasonable amount, and how you decided on it.

**Step 4.** Save your labeled example — your instructor will collect it, and you'll build on it in later lessons in this course.
