## Lesson Overview
This is the capstone of Course 17. Students step through an entire fictional client
engagement — introduction, discovery, proposal, execution, feedback, revision, and handoff —
using **Career Compass Club**, a fictional student-run school club, as the client. Every
document needed is provided in `Simulation_Resources/`. Nothing here requires or implies a
real client, real payment, or a real legally binding contract — this is stated explicitly to
students at the start (see Student_Lesson.md) and on every simulation document.

## Instructor Prep
- Read through all of `Simulation_Resources/` once before teaching — the fictional client
  (Priya Alvarez, faculty advisor of Career Compass Club) has a consistent voice across the
  inquiry email, discovery script, feedback, and revision request. You will be role-playing
  this client during the discovery call and feedback stages.
- Decide whether students work individually or in pairs.
- Print or share `Simulation_Resources/Sample_Completed_Solution.md` only AFTER grading, as a
  reference — not before, so it doesn't short-circuit students' own proposal-writing.

## Teaching Sequence (across 3 sessions)
| Session | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–15 min | Set the frame: this is a full simulation, fully fictional, safe to fail and iterate |
| 1 | 15–30 min | Distribute `Client_Inquiry_Email.md`; students read and note initial questions |
| 1 | 30–60 min | Run the Discovery Call as a role-play — instructor plays Priya using `Discovery_Call_Script.md`; students ask questions and take notes |
| 1 | 60–75 min | Students begin `Project_Requirements.md` (scope notes) individually |
| 2 | 0–30 min | Students draft their Proposal (using the Lesson 7 `Proposal_Template.md`) and `Pricing_Scope_Worksheet.md` |
| 2 | 30–45 min | Peer review: swap proposals, give one piece of feedback each |
| 2 | 45–75 min | Students revise and finalize their proposal; "submit" to instructor |
| 3 | 0–10 min | Instructor distributes `Client_Feedback.md` (approval + one requested change) to every student, playing the client's response |
| 3 | 10–40 min | Students execute the one requested change (a scope-appropriate revision, e.g. adjusting a page's content, not a full rebuild) and document it |
| 3 | 40–55 min | Students complete `Final_Handoff_Checklist.md` and write a short handoff message |
| 3 | 55–75 min | Reflection (`Reflection_Worksheet.md`) and instructor debrief using `Evaluation_Rubric.md` |

## Instructor Talking Points
- "Every real client engagement has these same beats: someone reaches out, you ask questions,
  you propose a plan, you deliver, they give feedback, you adjust, you hand off. Today you do
  all six, safely, in one class arc."
- Reinforce throughout: this is fictional, for practice — no real payment changes hands, and
  nothing here is a real legal contract.

## Common Misconceptions
- "The client's first message tells me everything I need." → Discovery questions matter; a
  vague inquiry always needs clarifying questions before a real proposal can be written.
- "Feedback means I did something wrong." → Model that even a strong first delivery normally
  gets at least one adjustment request — that's normal, not a failure.

## Likely Student Difficulties & Troubleshooting
| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Student proposal ignores discovery-call details | Have them re-read Discovery_Call_Script.md notes before revising |
| Scope creep during the "revision" stage | Point back to Pricing_Scope_Worksheet.md — is this change inside or outside original scope? |
| Student unsure how to respond to feedback professionally | Model a sample reply on the board using L7's communication norms |

## How to Review Student Work
Grade using `Evaluation_Rubric.md`. Look for: discovery notes that reflect the call, a
proposal matching the requirements, a revision that's professionally communicated, and a
completed handoff checklist.
