# Student Lesson: Marketplace Models — What Changes at Different Ages?

## What You'll Learn Today

By now in Course 17, you've practiced describing a skill, pricing your work, and pitching yourself. This lesson takes a step back and looks at a different question: **why do online marketplaces treat users differently depending on how old they are, and what does that actually mean for someone your age?**

We're going to study four real platforms — Fiverr, Upwork, Etsy, and Gumroad — as **case studies**, the same way you'd study four different countries' laws in a social studies class. We are not signing up for any of them today. This is about understanding how these systems are built, not about joining one.

## Why Do Marketplaces Have Age Rules at All?

It's not random. There are usually three real reasons behind it:

1. **Contracts and the law.** When you use a marketplace, you're agreeing to a legal contract (the Terms of Service). In most places, someone under 18 has limited legal ability to enter into contracts on their own. That's a real legal fact, not a company being difficult.
2. **Money and payments.** Getting paid through a platform usually involves banking and tax rules that are built around adults holding accounts. Someone has to be legally responsible for the money.
3. **Safety.** Marketplaces often involve messaging between strangers who don't know each other in real life. Platforms build in extra protection — like requiring a parent or guardian to be involved — because of that.

Different companies solve these problems in different ways. That's exactly what we're comparing today.

## Case Study: Four Platforms, Four Models

**Important caveat before you read this table:** these are real, general policies as commonly described at the time this lesson was written. **Companies change their Terms of Service.** If you ever seriously consider using one of these platforms, you must check that platform's *current* policy yourself — do not rely on a class handout from a past lesson.

| Platform | How it generally handles minors (13–17) | What's required |
|---|---|---|
| **Fiverr** | Can be used by 13–17 year-olds only through an account that is owned and managed by a parent or legal guardian. Full adult terms apply once you reach the age of majority (commonly 18). | A parent/guardian must own and control the account. |
| **Upwork** | Does not allow use by anyone under 18, or under the age of majority in their country if that's different from 18. | You must be an adult (by your local law) to use it at all. |
| **Etsy** | Can be used by 13–17 year-olds only through a shop account owned and operated by a parent or legal guardian. | A parent/guardian must own and control the account. |
| **Gumroad** | Age requirements, guardian involvement, and whether you can even receive payouts depend on your country and personal circumstances — there isn't one single global rule. | You'd need to check current, country-specific policy. |

Notice the pattern: two platforms (Fiverr, Etsy) build a path for younger teens *through* a parent or guardian's account. One platform (Upwork) simply says adults only. One platform (Gumroad) says "it depends where you live." None of these are wrong — they're just different business and legal choices.

## What We Do NOT Teach

This is important, so we're going to say it plainly:

> **Lumexa never teaches, suggests, or endorses misrepresenting your age or bypassing an age requirement on any platform.** Doing that can get an account permanently banned (along with any money in it), violates the platform's terms, and in some situations can create real legal problems. If a platform requires you to be a certain age, or requires a parent/guardian-owned account, the responsible path is either to wait, to involve a parent or guardian, or to choose a different avenue — never to lie about it.

This lesson exists so you *understand* these systems, not so you can find a way around them.

## Key Terms

- **Age of majority** — the age at which the law considers someone a legal adult, able to enter contracts on their own. It's 18 in most places you're likely to encounter, but it can differ by country.
- **Guardian-owned account** — an account that is legally held and controlled by a parent or legal guardian, even if a minor's work happens through it.
- **Terms of Service (ToS)** — the legal agreement you accept when you use a platform. It's a real contract, which is exactly why age and contract-capacity rules matter.
- **Payout** — the actual transfer of earned money from a platform to a person's bank account or payment method; often has its own separate rules about who can receive it.
